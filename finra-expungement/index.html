What a single-disclosure matter costs

Two of these three line items are not mine. FINRA sets its own forum fees by rule and assesses the expungement hearing sessions against the party requesting expungement. The court fee depends on where the confirmation petition is filed.

  • Legal fee, standard single-disclosure engagement Flat. Covers the statement of claim, arbitrator selection, the prehearing conference, the expungement hearing, and the petition to confirm the award. $1,000
  • FINRA filing fee Rule 13900(a)(3) requires an associated person requesting expungement to pay the Non-Monetary / Not Specified filing fee. $1,600
  • FINRA hearing session fees $1,150 per session under Rule 13902(a)(4), typically two to three sessions, less the one-session credit in the award. Assessed against the party requesting expungement. $1,150–$2,300
  • Court filing and service FINRA Rule 2080 requires a court to confirm the award before the disclosure is removed. Varies by forum. $400–$700
  • Estimated total, fee and costs together $4,150–$5,600

Forum fee amounts are those in effect under the FINRA fee schedules as of January 1, 2025 and are subject to change by FINRA. Costs are estimates, not a quote. A matter that needs additional hearing sessions, or a court that requires local counsel, will cost more. You are responsible for these costs whatever the outcome.

The filing deadlines, stated precisely

Straight-in expungement requests are time-barred under Rule 13805. The dates below come from the transition provision for disclosures that predate the October 2023 rule amendments. Whether any of them applies to your disclosure is a case-specific question.

  • October 16, 2026. For customer complaints initially reported to the CRD system on or before October 16, 2023, a straight-in request must be filed within three years of that date.
  • October 16, 2025, now passed. The corresponding two-year window for customer arbitrations and civil litigation that closed on or before October 16, 2023 has already run.
  • Six years. Rule 13206(a) eligibility applies separately and independently of the windows above.
  • Everything after October 2023. Disclosures arising from later matters run on their own clocks under Rule 13805(a)(2), not on the transition dates above.

How the process runs

  1. Eligibility review and engagement

    I look at the disclosure, the underlying matter, and the dates, and tell you whether a straight-in request is available and worth filing. Representation begins only after a conflict check and a signed engagement letter.

  2. Statement of claim

    The request is filed against the member firm where you were associated when the dispute arose. The filing fee is paid to FINRA at this stage.

  3. A three-arbitrator panel

    Since the October 2023 amendments, straight-in requests are decided by three public arbitrators drawn from FINRA's Special Arbitrator Roster, not by a single arbitrator.

  4. Recorded hearing

    You must appear in person or by video. The customer whose dispute is at issue is entitled to attend and participate, and state securities regulators may attend and participate as well.

  5. Award, then court confirmation

    If the panel recommends expungement it must explain its reasons in writing. Rule 2080 then requires a court of competent jurisdiction to confirm the award, with FINRA named as a party unless it waives, before the disclosure comes off CRD.

What a panel has to find

Expungement is not available because a disclosure is old, embarrassing, or commercially inconvenient. The panel must find at least one of the three grounds set out in FINRA Rule 2080:

  • the claim, allegation, or information is factually impossible or clearly erroneous;
  • you were not involved in the alleged investment-related sales practice violation, forgery, theft, misappropriation, or conversion of funds; or
  • the claim, allegation, or information is false.

If the facts of your disclosure do not fit one of those three, I will tell you that at the eligibility review rather than after you have paid a $1,600 filing fee.

Questions

Why is the legal fee smaller than the costs?

Because the costs are set by FINRA and the courts, not by me. A flat fee at this level is workable for a straightforward single-disclosure matter where the facts are clean and the firm does not contest. Matters that turn contested are quoted separately, and I will tell you before that happens rather than after.

Can you handle this if I am not in Florida or Ohio?

The FINRA arbitration itself is a private forum proceeding. The petition to confirm the award is a court filing, and I am admitted in Florida and Ohio. Where the confirmation has to be filed elsewhere, that step is handled with local counsel at additional cost, which you would know about before engagement.

Will my firm find out?

A straight-in request is filed against the member firm where you were associated when the dispute arose, so that firm is served and is a party. This is not a confidential process.

How long does it take?

Arbitrator selection, a prehearing conference, a hearing, an award, and a court petition are sequential steps in two different forums. Plan in quarters, not weeks. I will not give you a date certain, because neither FINRA's calendar nor the court's is mine to promise.

What happens if the panel says no?

The disclosure stays on your record and you have still paid the forum fees, because FINRA assesses hearing session fees against the party requesting expungement regardless of outcome. That is the risk of filing, and it is the reason the eligibility review comes first.
